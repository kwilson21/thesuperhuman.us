Release pages: alternatives

Five calls from the Hallmark audit about the Old News premiere (/music/old-news) and the Releases page (/audio/releases). Each shows what ships today, then two or three alternatives built from the site’s own fonts, colours and copy. One option per decision is marked Recommended, and Keep as is counts as a real choice.

Today’s renders are crops of the CI captures for PR #204 (f0abd1f) at 1280 and 390 wide. PR #204 keeps the Georgia face and the .93 line height and adds only a long-word size guard, so these regions match the PR 199 captures. Alternatives are live HTML recreations using the values in music-premiere.css, music-hubs.css and global.css as of PR #204, with the real poster and cover art. The premiere recreations keep the production masthead font stack, so their title face depends on your device (D8.1 is that choice). Nothing here changes the site, adds copy or adds motion. Gelasio loads from Google Fonts for this preview only; every other face is the site’s own self-hosted file.

How the picks fit together. D8.1 changes the masthead’s face, not its size or line height, so the D8.3 numbers hold for any face. D8.3-B sets the size through PR #204’s --masthead-size, so the long-word guard keeps working with whichever face D8.1 picks. Picking D8.2-D (lead column) also answers D8.3, so D8.3-B wouldn’t be needed. D8.4 is the only sitewide change; the rest stay on the two release pages.

D8.1 · Worth considering 10 · punch list #39 · audio-3

Which typeface should set the release masthead?

What Hallmark flagged. The largest type on /music/old-news is set in system Georgia at weight 900, outside the site’s type tokens. Georgia ships with Mac, Windows and iPhone but not Android or Linux, so the masthead changes by platform. Georgia has no 900 weight either, so where it exists the browser draws Georgia Bold.

Where today’s choice was decided. No doc records the face. It arrived with the approved editorial premiere (src/styles/music-premiere.css:1 and :7; docs/asset-reviews/old-news-premiere.md:6). The audit asks you to confirm whether the heavy newspaper weight was deliberate. Every option below keeps a heavy weight, the size, the .93 line height and PR #204’s long-word size guard. B and C change only the face; D also changes the case and the tracking.

D8.1-AKeep Georgia 900

Today
What changes
Nothing.
Why it could be better
Mac, Windows and iPhone visitors keep the Georgia Bold the premiere shipped with. No work, and nothing new to review.
Trade-off
At least three looks in the wild: Georgia Bold on Mac, Windows and iPhone, and different substitutes on Android and Linux (the CI capture above is one of them). The 900 asks for a weight Georgia doesn’t have. The family stays outside the type tokens.

D8.1-BLibron Bold

Recommended

Why this one: It fixes both findings with a font file the site already ships, and keeps a heavy front-page weight on every device.

What changes
.masthead-title uses font-family: var(--font-serif) at font-weight: 700. Nothing else changes.
Why it could be better
The same masthead on every device, inside the type tokens. Libron-Bold.woff2 is already in public/fonts/libron-v0.25/ and declared in global.css, so there’s no new font or licence. At this size it still reads as a newspaper headline.
Trade-off
A little rounder and lighter than Georgia Bold. If nothing else on the page uses bold Libron, the page fetches one more font file (about 80 KB). Libron is wider, so recheck PR #204’s long-word size guard at 320px.

D8.1-CGelasio Bold, self-hosted

Keeps today’s Georgia look
What changes

A third serif on the site (Libron, Newsreader on the agreement pages and the signed PDF, Gelasio) for one line of type, while Worth considering 5 is about cutting serifs, not adding them. Close to Georgia, not identical. One more file and licence to maintain.

D8.1-DRecord-sleeve italic

New voice, no new file
What changes
.masthead-title uses var(--font-serif) at 700 italic, drops text-transform: uppercase so the title shows in its own case, and tracks at -.04em. Size, line height and the size guard stay. The artist and credit lines keep their capitals and rules.

The nameplate reads like the title on a record sleeve rather than a newspaper headline, so the poster carries the newspaper idea and the type carries the music. D8.2 · Your call 16 · punch list #38 · audio-2

Should anything in the premiere masthead move off the centre line?

What Hallmark flagged. “Centred everything” (gate 6): the artist line, title, credit and both buttons share one centre line. Hallmark’s fix keeps the title centred and moves the button row or the artist line off centre. The verifier called it overstated, because the symmetry is a newspaper front page.

Where today’s choice was decided. docs/asset-reviews/old-news-premiere.md:6,8 (the approved editorial premiere mockup) and src/styles/music-premiere.css:1,6-9. A test that PR #204 adds pins the centred masthead (tests/pages/music-release-details.test.ts:23).

Recommended

D8.2-DLead column

Biggest change

Phones keep today’s single-column stack.

What changes
Artist line and title stay centred over a full-width rule. Below, a one-third column holds the credit, both buttons and the YouTube and SoundCloud links (moved up from under the poster). The poster takes the other two thirds.
Why it could be better
It’s how a front page sets a lead story beside its photo. Actions and credits read as one block, and the shorter poster fits almost entirely on a 1280 × 800 first screen (see D8.3).
Trade-off
The poster loses its full width and some drama. Moving the platform links changes the reading order. The poster renders at a new size, so its placement needs rechecking.

D8.3-BShorter masthead on short screens

Recommended

Why this one: It fixes the cut where it happens with one media query, and leaves the approved scale untouched at 1440 × 900, on large screens and on phones.

What changes
One media query for screens wider than 800px and 880px tall or less: the title size follows the screen height (--masthead-size: clamp(5rem, min(14.5vw, 15svh), 12.5rem), so PR #204’s long-word guard still caps it), the artist line uses its tablet size (1.2rem) and the button row padding tightens.
Why it could be better
The newspaper name and the poster’s play button land on the first screen at 1024 × 768, 1280 × 800 and 1536 × 864. The rule only applies where the problem is.
Trade-off
On short laptops the title is about a third smaller (186px to 120px at 1280 × 800), so the masthead dominates less there. At 1366 × 768 the poster’s play button is still 43px below the fold.

D8.4 · Your call 24 · punch list #58 · audio-21

Should the accent rule under action links sit closer to the text?

What Hallmark flagged. Decorative underlines 5px or more below the baseline (gate 35). Measured with Libron at 16px: action links (.site-link, such as “Explore release →”) draw their rule about 13px below the baseline, and their box is about 35px tall. Links inside paragraphs sit about 4px below and already pass.

Where today’s choice was decided. src/styles/global.css:108-113 (links) and :225 (.site-link) in PR #204; the audit cited :99-104 and :200 at 26e764a. The approved concepts draw one rule under the words and the arrow (docs/design-reviews/audio-2026-09-15/releases-approved.png, portfolio-approved.png). PR #204 already moves the /building tap areas onto a pseudo-element (Worth considering 24); this decision is about the house link everywhere else.

D8.4-BText underline

Sitewide restyle
What changes
Links and .site-link use text-decoration: underline 1px var(--accent) with text-underline-offset: .2em (about 3px). The border goes. Arrows in a span sit outside the underline. The two bare arrows on /audio/releases would need a span.
Why it could be better
The line belongs to the words. It breaks around descenders like typeset text, which is what Hallmark’s gate asks for.
Trade-off
Restyles every link on the site (about 60 action links in the markup, plus every link in running text). The arrow loses the shared rule from the concepts. To keep the arrow out of the underline the link becomes inline, and its tap area drops from about 35px to 27px.

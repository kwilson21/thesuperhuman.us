# Redesign story: source material and proposed publishing approach

September 10, 2026. Working editorial notes, not a published essay or a claim of
launch. The owner has added project continuity and a potential redesign writing
topic to the pre-launch scope. Deployment and live email tests remain on hold.

## Recommended homes

Use one private development journal for durable project decisions, evidence,
verification and next work. Present selected, audience-reviewed outcomes in a
Personal website project under Building. Publish a separate essay under Writing
when there is a coherent argument. Public design notes can accompany the project
milestones; they do not need another tracking system.

Working essay title: **A website that is easier to understand, and easier to
change.** A short potential-topic entry on Writing can identify this as in
progress and lead to actual project notes. Do not link to a nonexistent article,
invent a publication date, or present an unfinished essay as published.

The project journal answers what changed and why. The essay explains what can be
learned from the redesign. Reuse the evidence and link between them; avoid
maintaining two versions of project status or repeating the whole chronology.

## Story material from the conversation

Editorial direction, updated September 10: public website entries describe the
owner's intention at each step in concise narrative wording. Do not use direct
conversation quotes or attribution blocks. Ground the wording in recorded
decisions and let the studies and comparisons show what changed.

1. **The initial problem was manageability.** The request began with repository
   organization and consistent updates, without requiring a new visual identity.
   shadcn and SmoothUI were references for owned components, composition and
   discoverability, not requests to adopt their frameworks.
2. **Clarify intent before selecting components.** The owner redirected the
   process toward discussion, a written brief, an audit, and then a concrete
   model. The site needed to introduce a person, provide assessable professional
   evidence, and create room for compatible opportunities.
3. **The old site made readers assemble the picture.** The audit found repeated
   career history and AI philosophy, inconsistent navigation, no Writing index,
   and a stronger contracting pitch than the desired personal introduction.
   Its recorded word and scroll counts are measurements of that snapshot, not
   evidence that visitors were bored or abandoned the page.
4. **Give each area a job.** Home introduces and directs; Work owns professional
   evidence; Building owns projects; Writing develops ideas; About provides
   personal context. Audio remains connected through the secondary navigation.
5. **Show the idea before adding prose.** The owner wanted visuals, sound and
   motion to help explain complex work. The selected principle became shared
   structure with custom explanations, not one mandatory demonstration format.
6. **Keep the palette, change the composition.** Peace, curiosity and confidence
   informed the warm paper, ink and terracotta studies. Home direction A became
   the quiet studio. Direction D found a better purpose as About's personal path.
   The first mobile Home felt disjointed and was revised for a continuous surface.
7. **Editing includes subtraction.** Repeated explanations in the work-story
   study were removed. About became a brief journey instead of a second career
   history. The existing essay retained the argument that needed prose, with a
   custom explanatory figure and easier section access.
8. **A convincing image is not automatically trustworthy.** The owner rejected
   crowded, implausible audio equipment. The final direction retained the approved
   headphones and notebook, used service icons, and introduced checks for physical
   proportions, connections, reference fidelity and placement. Real screenshots
   represent software evidence; generated illustrations are labelled accordingly.
9. **Represent the work the owner wants to pursue.** The final positioning is
   Software engineer. Building with AI. Historical data/backend experience remains
   evidence. Public copy expresses interest in shaping solutions and compatible
   opportunities without exposing every private career uncertainty. A single
   general resume remains approval-gated.
10. **Specific facts still need human correction.** The owner clarified Lyft's
    three teams, supplied the quiet-hours example, and corrected fare recalculation
    to Associate Tools. A shared career source makes these corrections consistent.
11. **Maintainability became concrete conventions.** Astro, Tailwind and existing
    infrastructure stayed. Shared navigation, foundations, form behavior and a
    project layout serve recurring needs. Pages retain their custom explanations.
    No generic page-builder language or new frontend dependency was introduced.
12. **Review the connections as well as the pages.** Contact needed to open its
    disclosure at the destination. The repeated Lyft fragment needed to reopen a
    closed section. Keyboard, mobile, no-JavaScript and reduced-motion checks
    revealed behavior that approved pictures alone could not prove.
13. **Launch preparation is part of the design's credibility.** The runtime
    Turnstile-key mismatch was fixed and the release bundle checked in an isolated
    environment. These are local verification outcomes. Deployment, real delivery
    and visitor feedback are still separate steps.

For a first essay, select a few consequential decisions from this material rather
than turning every correction into a section. Strong visual candidates are the
original and revised navigation/content map, the rejected and revised mobile
composition, and the audio illustration-to-icons decision. Retain original dates
and label mockups separately from implemented screenshots.

## Journal and publication boundary

The owner selected a private local journal with a separate Cloudflare D1 recovery copy. The reviewed project-journal kit is delivered in an independent PR. Retrospective checkpoints record their actual recording date and describe historical work dates separately.

The public Personal website project contains eleven curated milestones and six page comparisons. Writing links to it as a potential essay topic. Neither page reads private journal files or credentials. Private records remain usable while the website is offline; rendering never writes a checkpoint or publishes an update. This prevents a runtime dependency cycle.

Raw conversation, prompts and private motivations stay out of public HTML and assets. Future feed publication uses the existing allowlist and audience review. Current notes are curated repository content, not new public-feed writes. See [delivery and launch gates](redesign-delivery.md) for remaining release work.

## Post-launch Threadline work

Use the published **Inspect before sending** entry as the reference. It pairs a
short explanation with a user-controlled illustration and a compact account of
what was checked. The text publication transport and the website's visual
attachment are separate responsibilities in the existing implementation.

Enrich existing entries in place, preserving IDs, original milestone dates,
current-status semantics and correction/withdrawal behavior. Select a screenshot,
annotated before/after, diagram or small interactive explanation for the specific
point. Do not manufacture historical screenshots or fill space with decorative
media. Where useful evidence is absent, record that gap and retain a clear text
entry until appropriate material exists. New explanatory reconstructions must be
labelled as such and must not imply public availability or measured improvement.

First inspect one older milestone alongside the approved live example, then
apply the agreed treatment to the remaining entries. Review public disclosures
and direct asset access, including any collapsed sections. Keep detailed source
verification private.
